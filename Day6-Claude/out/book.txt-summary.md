- Cells are the fundamental units of life, capable of performing essential biological processes such as obtaining energy, reproducing, and passing genetic information to future generations, with the cell theory establishing that all organisms are composed of cells arising from pre-existing cells.

- The chemical foundations of cellular life depend on key elements (carbon, hydrogen, oxygen, nitrogen, phosphorus, sulfur) and water, which serves as a solvent, regulates temperature, and participates in critical chemical reactions like hydrolysis and condensation.

- Biological molecules are categorized into four major groups—carbohydrates (energy sources and structural support), lipids (energy storage and membrane formation), proteins (diverse functions including catalysis and transport), and nucleic acids (genetic information storage and expression)—each with distinctive structures and functions essential to cellular operations.

- The plasma membrane surrounds every human cell and defines the cellular boundary, separating internal cellular components from the external environment while controlling what enters and exits the cell.
